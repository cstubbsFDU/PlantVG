Background

Why Stalks Fail

Stalk lodging is when wind bends a stalk until it breaks or kinks near the base, before harvest. Lodged plants are hard or impossible to harvest.

In bending tests, stalks fail in two ways: the tissue itself breaks, or the cross-section flattens and folds, called Brazier buckling.

An illustration of the two failure modes, not a fitted model.

The problem

We Need 3D Cells. We Have Two Flat Slides.

Micro-CT and confocal microscopes image cells in 3D, but they are costly and scarce. Almost every lab has a light microscope, which shows one flat cut at a time.

Cut the block two ways to see what a microscope slide captures.

PlantVG works backwards: from one cut of each kind, it builds a 3D block whose cuts look like the slides.

The plan

From Pixels to Voxels

A pixel is a square of an image. A voxel is a cube of a volume. Stack image slices and every pixel becomes a voxel.

Draw a cell outline on the grid, then give it depth.

Step 2 of 8: the transverse slide

Find and Trace Every Cell

Holes are filled, touching white pixels are grouped into cells, and a boundary walk traces each cell's outline. Every outline becomes one tube in the model.

Select a cell to watch the boundary walk go around it.

Step 3 of 8: the longitudinal slide

Measure How Long the Cells Are

Each cell's length is the height of its outline. Cells cut off by the top or bottom edge are dropped, because their real length is unknown.

Point at a cell to see its length. Then trim outliers with the range.

Step 4 of 8: statistics

Fit a Length Distribution

A handful of measured cells becomes a probability distribution. PlantVG draws new cell lengths from it, so every model is different but statistically alike.

With only a few cells, several distributions fit about equally well. The choice is prior knowledge, not proof from one slide.

Step 5 of 8: end caps

Walk Up Each Cell Column, Dropping End Caps

Each traced cell is a column of stacked cells. The first end cap lands at a random fraction of the average length, so neighbors don't line up. Each next cap is one drawn length further on.

Step 6 of 8: end-cap fillets

Round the Corners Where Caps Meet Walls

Real end caps curve into the side walls. PlantVG builds that curve by eroding two masks slice by slice near each cap: the cell interior shrinks, and the outer wall face pulls in.

The rounding starts this many slices from the cap: half the wall thickness plus the radius.

Step 7 of 8: building the matrix

Add the Cells One Tube at a Time

Each traced outline grows outward into a wall, runs through every slice, and closes at its own end caps with fillets. All tubes land in one shared voxel array.

Each color is one traced cell, matching the map on the right.

Step 8 of 8: output

Crop, Downsample, and Save a TIFF Stack

Extra slices are built at the start and cropped off, because the cap walk hasn't settled there. Edges are trimmed by the wall thickness plus radius. The result saves as a multi-page TIFF that ImageJ and FIJI open directly.
